Is this plan good? Yes.

Every muscle gets hit about twice a week with the right reps (8–12 big lifts, 10–15 small). Weekly sets: chest 14, back 12, biceps 10, triceps 10, side shoulders 7, abs 15, legs 15, calves 5. That's the range that builds muscle.

What I changed for your goals: arms, shoulders and abs were a bit light, so I added one set to Thursday curls, pushdowns and lateral raises, Friday lateral raises, and Saturday cable crunches. Nothing else moved.

Calves: moved off the calf station onto the leg press, right after your leg press sets, so you never have to go over there. Added 2 sets on Saturday so they get worked twice a week like everything else.

From Raj & Roh's workouts: their split (each muscle twice a week) matches yours, so the plan stays. Three swaps from their ab and arm routines: lying leg raises on Wednesday for lower abs, Bayesian cable curls on Thursday for the biceps peak, and side cable crunches instead of planks on Saturday for the V-cut.

Abs: they're a muscle. Load the crunch machine and cable crunch and add weight like any lift. Don't cut calories to see them; at 120 lb you need to eat to grow.

Arms and chest: the sets are there now. What makes them grow is adding reps or weight every week or two. Log it.

Not sore? That's fine. Soreness doesn't mean growth. What counts is how hard the sets are: the last 2–3 reps should be a real struggle with clean form. On machines, cables and curls, take the last set until you can't do another clean rep.

How to get stronger: pick a weight you can do with clean form, stopping 1–3 reps before you'd fail. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set (e.g. 12/12/12), go up one weight step next time.

Rest: 2–3 min on big lifts (presses, rows, pulldown, leg press, RDL), 1–2 min on small ones. Being sore isn't the goal; adding reps or weight is.

Food: about 250 calories over maintenance and 90–110 g protein a day. Aim to gain 0.25–0.5 lb a week. Sleep 7–9 hours.

Machine taken? Do the next lift and come back to it, or use the swap listed under the lift.
