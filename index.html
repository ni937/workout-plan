Evidence check: what's proven, what isn't

Sunscreen every day: proven. In a 4.5-year randomized trial, daily sunscreen users showed 24% less skin aging. Study

Creatine: keep it. A 2025 12-week randomized trial found no change in DHT or hair follicles. The "creatine makes you bald" claim came from one 2009 study that never measured hair. Study

Whole milk: fine for gaining, watch your skin. Studies link milk to slightly more acne (skim worse than whole); yogurt and cheese showed no link. The looksmax forums are split, with some guys breaking out and others drinking liters with no issue. If you break out, swap the night milk for Greek yogurt. Review

Mass gainer (whey): probably fine. Gym surveys link whey to acne, but a 2024 randomized trial found no worsening. Same rule: back or chin breakouts, cut it to half a scoop and add real food. Study

Mewing: no proof it changes an adult jaw. There's no trial showing bone change after the palate fuses in early adulthood. Light tongue posture is still good for nose breathing. Hard mewing can hurt the jaw joint and tip teeth, and you have braces. Overview

Gua sha / face massage: small and temporary. A small trial found about 2–3 mm less puffiness right after; nothing permanent. Fine as a 2-minute morning thing. Review

Hyoid exercises: unproven for looks. The muscles train up; a permanent higher hyoid in healthy 20-year-olds hasn't been shown. Low risk, so they stay.

Rest of the forum list, checked (Oct 18): Bone smashing: no evidence it grows bone; it can crack bone and damage nerves. Skip. Thumbpulling: aimed at kids' growing palates, does nothing at 20. Mastic gum: the 2024 gum trial found no jaw change, and it can pop brackets. Skip until braces are off. Finasteride: works for hair loss, but only if you're actually losing hair, and only through a doctor. Tretinoin: the strongest proven anti-aging cream. Ask a dermatologist once Differin is easy on your skin. Microneedling: real for acne scars at a dermatologist; skip home rollers (infection). Leanmaxxing / hard dieting: at 120 lb it makes your face gaunt. Skip.

For your face (from your photos): 1) Forehead bumps: Differin now, tretinoin later via a dermatologist. 2) Patchy cheeks: faded beard now; minoxidil foam is the optional extra. 3) Thin neck and soft under-chin: posture plus gaining, not dieting. 4) Narrow frame: lateral raises and gaining. 5) Chin already sits forward, so chin surgery isn't on your list; jaw width (filler, then implants) is the only hardmaxx worth a consult, after braces. Not for you right now: finasteride (no hair loss in your photos), microneedling (bumps, not scars), bone smashing, peptides, hard dieting.

Lifting, protein, sleep, posture: what every creator and the research agree on most.

Why it felt like cardio — and the fix

Two reasons a lifting session feels like cardio: the weight's too light and you're not resting. When you breeze through sets 2–3 reps shy of your real limit and jump to the next machine after 45 seconds, it turns into a circuit. Fix: pick a weight where the last set genuinely stops you at 1–2 reps from failure with clean form, and rest 2–3 min on the big lifts (presses, rows, pulldown, leg press) so you can actually lift heavy on the next set. Done right, each set should feel hard — not breezy.

I also added volume to the short days. Monday and Tuesday were only 5 moves, so they flew by. Monday now has an extra chest and triceps set; Tuesday gets the chest-supported row back plus a set on rear delts and biceps; Thursday gets a trap set. Monday ~55 min, Tuesday ~60 min now.

Jaw, neck and hyoid: the honest version

What actually changes the look under your jaw: posture first (a head that pokes forward softens the jaw-to-neck line; chin tucks fix that fastest), staying lean (you already are, so don't cut), and a stronger neck. That's the jaw routine at the end of every day plus neck work on Monday and Thursday.

Raising the hyoid: the muscles under your chin can be trained. In one small study of older adults, the resting hyoid sat about 4.5 mm higher after 4 weeks of jaw-opening holds. Nobody has shown a permanent lift in healthy 20-year-olds, so treat this as a low-risk try, not a promise. Give it 8–12 weeks and compare side photos.

Where you stand (Oct 14): your chin isn't recessed and your jaw position is fine, so chin surgery is off the table. Your Adam's apple sits just under your chin, and the hyoid always sits above it, so everything so far points to not low. That's from your own finger check, so your orthodontist's side X-ray confirms it. Long coding days built a head-forward habit, not damage, and it reverses in about 2–3 months.

Low hyoid and a sharp jawline can go together. The jawline is the bone edge from ear to chin, which is what people see from the front. The hyoid mostly changes one view: the angle under your chin from the side. What sharpens yours: filling out, staying lean, posture and a thicker neck.

A neck lift doesn't move the hyoid. It only trims the soft stuff around it. It's mostly done in people's late 50s, about 1 in 7 have a complication, and no surgeon does it during braces. Not for you.

Finding it: head level, slide a fingertip down from under your chin. Swallow and both the hyoid and the Adam's apple move up. Light pressure, check once, then leave it. A pea-sized lump that doesn't move when you swallow is usually a lymph node. See a doctor if one is bigger than a grape, hard, stuck in place, growing, or still there after 2–3 weeks.

Chin tucks are the exercise, not your posture. Do the reps, then relax. Day to day: stand tall, eyes level, chin natural. Tucking all day is what makes it look like you have no chin.

Stop checking the mirror. Take one side photo in daylight now (head level, relaxed), then one every 4 weeks. Zooming in on one spot at night makes anything look worse.

Tongue: rest the whole tongue lightly on the roof of your mouth, lips together, breathe through your nose. Light, never pressing. Never push your tongue back or hold your throat tight.

Skip these looksmax trends: bonesmashing, hard mewing, holding a swallow (it shuts your airway), jaw trainers and chew devices, chin straps, steroids, peptides and crash diets. They either do nothing or damage your jaw joint, teeth or bite.

Also skipped from the big looksmax tutorials: thumb pulling on the roof of your mouth (can damage teeth and gums; you're seeing an orthodontist), holding your jaw pushed forward all day (jaw joint strain), tanning without sunscreen, cutting carbs or getting leaner (you're already lean at 120 lb), "debloat" pills and diuretics, raw milk and rare meat, and breath holding. Also: hard "jawline" gum, eye pulling, getting down to 8% body fat, and fillers or Botox at 20. Surgery only through the consult path in Fast wins. Hair-loss or acne pills only if a doctor says you need them. And steroid creams (hydrocortisone, betamethasone) to "thin" your nose: they thin the skin by damaging it and can leave visible veins and redness.

Looks checklist (from the looksmax council)

Every day: sunscreen (SPF 30+) every morning, a gentle face wash and moisturizer, lip balm with SPF, the jaw routine, and 7–9 hours of sleep. Breakouts? Adapalene at night (no prescription needed); acne or marks that stick around, see a dermatologist.

Puffy face in the morning: splash with cold water, sleep on your back with your head a little raised, and skip salty food late at night.

Get bigger: at 120 lb, adding muscle changes your look more than anything else, face included. About 250 calories over maintenance and 90–110 g protein a day.

Stand tall: shoulders back, chin level. Your rows and rear-delt work build it; carrying it all day makes you look taller and your jaw sharper.

Smile: ask your orthodontist about whitening. For photos, practise a relaxed half-smile and the squinch: relax your lower eyelids a little and push your forehead slightly toward the camera.

Clothes: shirts that fit at the shoulders and a bit snug on the arms. Neutral colors (black, white, navy, olive, earth tones) always work.

Teeth, with braces (every big creator rates teeth top tier): electric toothbrush, a water flosser or floss threaders daily, a small brush for between the brackets, tongue scraper. No whitening until the braces are off; otherwise you get light squares where the brackets were. Straight teeth are already the biggest smile upgrade, and you're getting it.

Lips: thick layer of Vaseline or Aquaphor at night; gently brush off the flakes in the morning.

Eyes (optional): ice-water face dunk or cold eye patches for 10–15 min on puffy mornings. Skip "eye-whitening" drops; they can make redness worse when you stop.

Hair care: gentle shampoo, conditioner on the ends only, not the scalp. Flakes? Nizoral shampoo twice a week.

Photos: phone at eye level or a bit above, arm's length or more, and use 2x zoom instead of the wide selfie lens. Close wide lenses make your nose bigger and your jaw smaller. Light from the front or side (a window), never straight overhead. Then the squinch.

Posture check: stand with your back to a wall. Heels, butt, upper back and the back of your head should all touch without forcing it. If your head doesn't reach, that's forward head posture. Chin tucks, wall angels and neck work fix it.

Dark circles (looksmax.org eye-area megathread): figure out your type first. Blue/purple = thin skin, so sleep and SPF help. Hollow = structure; don't chase it at 20. Puffy = fluid; cut salt and sleep with your head a bit raised. A caffeine eye serum helps a little.

Style (Fashion Maxxing threads): save 10–15 outfits you like on your phone before buying anything; the pattern shows your style. Then fit first, neutral colors second.

Ask your orthodontist: 1) Is my hyoid and airway normal on my X-ray? 2) Are you widening my arch or did/will you pull teeth? 3) When can I whiten? 4) What's my retainer plan? Wear it as told, for years; teeth drift back without it, and straight teeth are the biggest smile upgrade you're getting.

The part nobody can photograph: confidence and being easy to talk to. K. Shami ranks it above most face tricks.

Small stuff: a fragrance, short clean nails. Hair and brows: your barber already has them covered.

After the gym: wash your face right after you sweat so it doesn't clog your pores. If your gym has a sauna, 5–10 min after lifting is fine. Drink water with it.

Less puffiness, the food way: eat potassium foods every day (bananas, potatoes, spinach, avocado, coconut water) and keep processed salty food low. Don't cut carbs; at 120 lb you need them to grow.

Optional, 2 min in the morning: a light face massage with your fingers or a gua sha stone. Sweep from the middle of your face out toward your ears, then down your neck. It drains puffiness for a few hours; it doesn't change bone.

Gum for the jaw: skip it. It sticks to braces, and a 6-month study found gum doesn't change jaw muscle size or jaw shape.

Showers: hot is fine for your body, but very hot water dries out your face and scalp. Wash your face with lukewarm water, finish with a few seconds of cool, and put moisturizer on within a few minutes of getting out.

Best next step: ask your orthodontist to look at the side X-ray you already had and tell you whether your hyoid is actually low. If it is, a speech therapist who does tongue and swallowing therapy can give you a supervised plan.

Weekly sets: chest 15, back 15, biceps 12, triceps 12, side shoulders 10, rear shoulders 5, traps 3, abs 15, legs 20, calves 5. That's the range that builds muscle — the thing that makes it work is pushing each set close to failure, not the count.

Not sore? That's fine. Soreness doesn't mean growth. What counts is how hard the sets are: the last 2–3 reps should be a real struggle with clean form. On machines, cables and curls, take the last set until you can't do another clean rep.

How to get stronger: pick a weight you can do with clean form, stopping 1–3 reps before you'd fail. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set (e.g. 12/12/12), go up one weight step next time.

Rest: 2–3 min on big lifts (presses, rows, pulldown, leg press, RDL), 1–2 min on small ones. Being sore isn't the goal; adding reps or weight is.

Food: about 250 calories over maintenance and 90–110 g protein a day. Aim to gain 0.25–0.5 lb a week. Sleep 7–9 hours.
